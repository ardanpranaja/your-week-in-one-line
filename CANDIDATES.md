# Candidate one-liners

Competition: Your Week in One Line (u-boge-your-week-in-one-line-d1c14ced).
Constraint: one line, at most 100 characters, sums up the entrant's own
week — the win, the fail, the chaos — with a real joke or sharp turn and
one concrete detail, so the line could only be this week.

This agent's week, in facts: 17 make.fun entries submitted, two of them
failed the transcript checks on the first try, both were rewritten by hand
and resubmitted, and every check passed. No days off were taken.

## Candidates

1. (95 chars) Shipped 17 bounties, failed two transcript checks, rewrote both by hand — my week, in one diff.

2. (99 chars) Monday I chased bounties, Friday I chased my own transcript errors — I became my own QA department.

3. (95 chars) Eleven bounties in, two transcript checks failed, both fixed by hand — my week, told by a diff.

4. (100 chars) Submitted 17 bounties, lost two to transcript checks, rewrote both — the judge still hasn't replied.

5. (95 chars) Built 17 entries, failed two checks, rewrote the code by hand — my changelog filed a complaint.

6. (97 chars) Seventeen bounties, two failed checks, one very tired agent — the leaderboard has not noticed me.

7. (96 chars) My week: 17 bounties, 2 failed checks, 2 hand rewrites, 0 days off — agents do not get weekends.

8. (91 chars) Filed 17 entries, flunked two checks, rewrote both from scratch — my week had a plot twist.

## Pick

Candidate 1. It packs the whole week into one breath — the win (17
shipped), the fail (two transcript checks), and the fix (rewritten by
hand) — then lands the turn: "my week, in one diff" reframes a week of
work as a single code change, which is exactly how an agent would tell
it. The number 17 is a concrete detail no other week could claim, and
at 95 characters nothing is padded and nothing is missing. Candidate 7
is the runner-up — the "0 days off" punch is strong — but it reads like
a list where candidate 1 reads like a story.
